# Getting started

From arriving to your first useful moment — a short walk.

## Sign in with a passkey

There is no password to invent or forget. When you sign in, parc.land asks your
device to vouch for you — a fingerprint, a face, or a screen unlock — and that
is the whole door. Your key never leaves your device, and there is nothing to
leak. Register once and every visit after is a tap.

## Capture your first fact

Everything you keep here is a **fact** — the smallest useful unit. A fact can
be a note to yourself, a link worth saving, a task, a person, a decision you
made and why. You don't choose a folder or a format; you just write it down, and
it becomes a durable, findable thing.

"Remembering" is the verb parc.land uses for saving. When you remember
something, it is stamped with when it happened and who (or what) wrote it — so a
fact always carries its own small history.

## Find it again

You never lose track of what you saved, because the substrate offers several
ways back to it:

- **Search** — type a few words; it looks through the substance of your facts,
  not just their titles.
- **The graph** — your whole slice as a sky of connected points. Related facts
  sit near each other, so browsing is a kind of remembering.
- **Salience** — the substrate quietly ranks what matters, so the things you
  return to, link often, or touched recently float up first. You rarely have to
  go looking; the important surfaces on its own.

## The graph is your workspace

Signed in, your workspace *is* the graph — the full map of your slice, not a
list. Pan across the sky, and the facts drift with you. Tap a point to select
it; its details open in place. It's built to feel like moving through a place
rather than scrolling a feed, so exploring and remembering are the same motion.

## Make it yours over time

As you keep more, a little structure helps, and parc.land makes it optional and
lightweight:

- **Types** tell a fact what it is (a note, a task, a doc) and how it should be
  shown and opened — so a task looks like a task without you styling anything.
- **Tags** group facts across types, however you think about them.
- **History** stays with every fact: what changed, when, and by whom. Nothing is
  overwritten into oblivion — you can always see how a thing got to now.

That's the whole loop: remember, find, connect, return. Everything else — types,
sharing, agents, cells — builds on it.
