# Round eight: the round seven studies, worked in order (23 November)

The five changes, in the order agreed:
1. **Two values a form, split by one edge.** Figure strokes take their light from a hard terminator, broken a little by the bristles, instead of a smooth gradient. The per-stroke random value is nearly gone (value 0.98–1.02, down from 0.94–1.06), so the light makes the difference, not the stroke.
2. **The garment's own shape.** The top is close at the shoulder, looser down the body and fullest where it blouses over a hem band. Sleeves fill out toward the forearm and gather at a cuff band. A trouser leg hangs straight from the knee, wider than the shin toward the ankle.
3. **The drape.** Strokes fan from each shoulder point down across the front and back toward the other hip, sagging as they go. They replace the strokes that ran along the bone on the top.
4. **Face planes.** The sockets are a band of shade under the brow, plus the nose's shaded side and a short touch for the mouth, with no eyes. The face's normals are flattened toward its front, so the light splits it side to side, as a turning cheek does, rather than across the mouth (which read as a beard). Loose strands lift off the hair's edge. Below the ears, the head's block-in is shaded skin.
5. **Accents and secondary motion.** The sailor collar is larger (in character.ts). Skirt and coat columns are pushed outward toward the hem (up to 20%), so the skirt swings and the coat streams.

## Then: every part painted the new way
- `cast-all-splines.jpg`: the cast with nothing on the figure left as an old stamp.
- `hands-shoes.jpg`: the teen's hands, the schoolgirl's loafers and socks, and the runner's shoe in profile.

Hands are one shape (the palm through the fingers to their tips) plus the thumb, over the cuff. Shoes are painted along the foot, heel to toe: the top, two sides, a darker sole, a toe cap, and an ankle stroke down into the shoe from inside the trouser leg. Anything else of the body gets panels round its own block. The contact line under a gripping hand, the skirt's pleats and the ponytail's flick are drawn as splines. The old stamp figure (the torso and limb pulls, the back pull, the seat, the wraps) is now painted only where splines aren't available; the ground contact shadows stay as stamps. A unit test checks that every character is painted wholly in splines.
