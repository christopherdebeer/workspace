# Percept

Local time-reversal of short audio segments — a perceptual experiment turned into a controllable medium.

## What you hear

When successive short slices of a sound are played backwards and then joined in original order, the ear/brain can still recover the original message provided the slices stay short enough. For speech this window is typically around 20–50&nbsp;ms; longer grains produce an uncanny or fully abstract texture. The classic demonstration is Saberi & Perrott (*Nature*, 1999).

This experiment lets you set that temporal grain yourself and hear the transition from transparent restoration → uncanny → collapse.

## Controls

- **Grain size** (10–200&nbsp;ms) — the length of each reversed segment.
- **Play original / Play processed** — direct comparison.
- **Low / Mid / High** checkboxes — reserved for future band-selective reversal (currently all bands are treated together).
- Load any short audio file, or use the built-in synthetic tone / noise demos.

The waveform view shades every other grain so you can see the physical structure while the ear may still hear something coherent.

## Creative use

Because restoration is a perceptual act, the same physical output can feel different after a few listens or when attention is directed. This makes local reversal useful as a generative or compositional tool:

- keep a source’s identity while scrambling its micro-structure;
- create material that “unlocks” on repeated listening;
- drive grain size from external signals (envelope, movement, time of day) for evolving soundscapes.

## Design questions

Does seeing the reversed blocks change how the restoration feels? At what grain size does a given source stop being “itself”? Can the transition itself become the aesthetic content of a piece?
