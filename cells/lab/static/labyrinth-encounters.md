# Labyrinth: encounters

A thought exercise, 20 October 2026, following Astra's proposal: pair pieces at random, at random sides (above, below, diagonal), with the action that brought them together, and reason, blind to what the engine can do and as far as possible to its vocabulary, about what would delight a player if the system were perfectly expressive. Then, only afterwards, what each delight would take; then a third element added to some, to be kept.

The draws are seeded, so they're honest: 24 encounters (`seed 20261020`), from 28 pieces, 12 relations and 8 actions; and 6 of them drawn again with a third element (`seed 777`). Some draws are odd (a window against an arcade, a stair wrapping a tower "placed onto" it); where a relation can't be taken literally, the nearest physical reading is said first, in italics.

For each: what stays recognisable; then three imagined outcomes: the **obvious** satisfying one, the **surprising** plausible one, and the **smallest** sufficient one (often just a good joint); and the verbs (Astra's: *merge, open, carry, wrap, step, shelter, frame, yield, remain separate*).

## Stage 1: twenty-four encounters, read blind

**01 · terrace raised beside a stair flight, the flight inside it.** *A terrace raised so the flight ends up within its footprint.* Recognisable: the flight still climbs; the terrace is still a level place.
- Obvious: the terrace's surface opens as a stairwell round the flight, a guard round the opening; the flight arrives on the terrace, which becomes its landing.
- Surprising: the terrace is hollow over the lower flight: a vaulted, covered stair under it, coming up through an arched opening into the light.
- Smallest: the terrace stops a stair's width short, leaving the flight in a slot with a low parapet along it.
- Verbs: yield, open, step, shelter. Caused by: the stair was there first; the terrace made room exactly round it.

**02 · dome placed on to a pergola.** Recognisable: the pergola's open frame; a dome.
- Obvious: the frame strengthens into a ring of posts and a ring beam, and the dome sits on it: an open pavilion, the pergola's vines climbing on up the ring.
- Surprising: the dome takes the pergola's language: an open ribbed cupola, dappled light through it.
- Smallest: the dome over the centre bay only, the rest of the pergola open beams round it, the dome its crown.
- Verbs: carry, shelter, frame. Caused by: a light frame asked to carry something heavy, staying open while it does.

**03 · a house, again and again, against a well, wrapping round it.** Recognisable: the well, with room to draw water; the houses.
- Obvious: the houses gather round, leaving a small well court; their doors face the well; a paved apron round it.
- Surprising: the nearest house opens its ground floor into a loggia over the well: half in, half in the court, a roof's edge sheltering whoever draws water.
- Smallest: the house's corner chamfered back so the well keeps a full circle of space.
- Verbs: wrap, yield, frame, shelter. Caused by: repetition read as gathering round a centre; the well becomes why they're arranged so.

**04 · a window put against an arcade's side.** *A window placed on an open bay.*
- Obvious: the bay closes into a screen wall with the window in it: one bay of the arcade becomes a room's front.
- Surprising: the window goes up a storey: the arcade gains an upper room over it, looking out where the window was put.
- Smallest: the bay gets a low sill and balustrade: it reads as a window on to the outside without being walled.
- Verbs: frame, yield, shelter. Caused by: a window implies an inside.

**05 · a court, then a tower added overlapping its end.**
- Obvious: the tower becomes the court's corner tower: the walls run into it, a door from the court into its foot, its top looking over the court.
- Surprising: the tower becomes the gatehouse: the way into the court passes through its ground floor.
- Smallest: the court's wall thickens where it meets the tower, and nothing else changes.
- Verbs: merge, open, frame. Caused by: overlapping the boundary, the tower becomes part of the boundary.

**06 · an earth bank drawn toward a buttress, through its foot.**
- Obvious: the buttress holds the bank: the bank's face steps against it, a buttressed retaining wall.
- Surprising: the buttress's foot becomes an opening, a small culvert or a passage through the bank under the buttress.
- Smallest: earth heaped against the buttress at its natural slope, planting in the angle.
- Verbs: carry, merge, yield. Caused by: a support meeting a load it can bear.

**07 · a stair flight, then a pier added through it at an angle.**
- Obvious: the flight turns round the pier, which becomes the newel at a small landing.
- Surprising: the pier carries a canopy over the flight: a covered stair.
- Smallest: the pier moves to the flight's edge and becomes the post of its rail.
- Verbs: yield, carry, step, shelter. Caused by: an upright in a stair is a newel.

**08 · a colonnade lowered into a basin cascade, end to end.**
- Obvious: the colonnade runs beside the cascade, its columns stepping down with the basins: a stepped colonnade along a water stair.
- Surprising: its last columns stand in the lowest basin, reflected: a portico in the water.
- Smallest: the colonnade ends at the cascade's head in a bay overlooking it.
- Verbs: step, frame, wrap. Caused by: both have a rhythm; they share it.

**09 · a pier drawn through a colonnade's run.**
- Obvious: the pier becomes a stronger support dividing the run into bays: a pier and columns rhythm.
- Surprising: the pier rises above the colonnade into a small lantern or bell-cote marking its middle.
- Smallest: the pier takes the place of a column, in step, only thicker.
- Verbs: merge, frame. Caused by: the pier punctuates.

**10 · a pool raised on top of an arcade.**
- Obvious: the arcade's top becomes a water terrace, spouts through the spandrels dropping into a channel below.
- Surprising: at one end the water falls as a curtain between two arches: a walk behind a waterfall.
- Smallest: the pool on the arcade with a rim, one spout.
- Verbs: carry, step (for water), frame. Caused by: water held high wants to come down, and the arcade is how.

**11 · a bridge grown toward a cypress, side to side.**
- Obvious: the bridge's parapet bows out round the tree: a bay on the deck in its shade, a seat.
- Surprising: the tree comes up through the deck, a ring round its trunk.
- Smallest: a gap in the parapet where the branches pass.
- Verbs: yield, wrap, frame. Caused by: the tree was there; the bridge made room.

**12 · a stair raised a cell short of a gate.**
- Obvious: the stair's foot meets the gate across a short landing: it leads through.
- Surprising: the stair climbs on to the gate: its arch becomes a lookout, a walk over it.
- Smallest: the gap left, paved, a small forecourt shared by the stair's foot and the gate.
- Verbs: step, merge, remain separate. Caused by: near things want to be joined; or to share a place.

**13 · a thick wall raised beside a court, end to end.**
- Obvious: the wall becomes the court's back wall, taller, with niches or a fountain on its court face.
- Surprising: the wall's thickness becomes inhabited: a gallery or rooms in it, looking into the court.
- Smallest: it meets the court's corner cleanly, the coping running on.
- Verbs: merge, frame, shelter. Caused by: a thick wall beside an enclosure is its boundary.

**14 · a thick wall lowered on to a cypress.**
- Obvious: an arched niche round the trunk, the tree growing through the wall.
- Surprising: the wall splits and the tree stands in a small court between its halves.
- Smallest: the wall stops either side of the tree, its ends finished.
- Verbs: yield, frame, open. Caused by: the tree kept; the wall adapts.

**15 · a stair flight, then a terrace added overlapping its end.**
- Obvious: the terrace becomes the flight's top landing; the flight arrives through a gap in its parapet.
- Surprising: the terrace overlaps mid-flight, and the stair becomes two flights with the terrace a half-landing between: a place to stop and look.
- Smallest: the terrace notched round the last step.
- Verbs: merge, step, open. Caused by: a level surface meeting an inclined one becomes a landing.

**16 · a balcony lowered on to a rock outcrop.**
- Obvious: the balcony rests on the rock, the rock its bracket: a natural belvedere.
- Surprising: it carries on into a cave in the rock: a grotto behind it.
- Smallest: its edge follows the rock's line; a step down on to the rock.
- Verbs: carry, merge, step. Caused by: rock is support.

**17 · a bridge, then a buttress added against it at an angle.**
- Obvious: the buttress braces the bridge's pier, a cutwater.
- Surprising: its top becomes a refuge on the deck: a small bay with a seat over the drop.
- Smallest: it hugs the bridge's side under the deck.
- Verbs: carry, frame. Caused by: support offered becomes a place.

**18 · a pier, then a buttress added overlapping its end.**
- Obvious: one stepped support: a buttressed pier.
- Surprising: the pair becomes the foot of a flying arch between them.
- Smallest: the buttress thickens the pier's base.
- Verbs: merge, carry.

**19 · a bridge drawn toward a balcony, corner to corner.**
- Obvious: the bridge's end turns to meet the balcony square on; the balcony widens into a landing.
- Surprising: the corner meeting becomes an angled bay, a little polygonal landing.
- Smallest: the bridge's end chamfered, a short slab joining the corners.
- Verbs: merge, step. Caused by: an awkward meeting becomes a place.

**20 · a well, then a dome added overlapping its side.**
- Obvious: a well-house: the well under a small dome on columns.
- Surprising: an oculus over the well: light falling down the shaft.
- Smallest: the dome beside, with a niche; the well open to the sky.
- Verbs: shelter, frame.

**21 · a terrace, then a well added inside it.**
- Obvious: a well-head on the terrace drawing from below: the terrace a cistern's roof.
- Surprising: a spout at the terrace's face, the cistern's overflow.
- Smallest: a well-head with an apron, the parapet kept clear of it.
- Verbs: merge, frame.

**22 · an earth bank placed on to a rock outcrop.**
- Obvious: the bank drapes the rock, crags showing through, planted.
- Surprising: the rock becomes rough steps up the bank.
- Smallest: earth fills only its hollows.
- Verbs: merge, step.

**23 · a dome drawn through a window.**
- Obvious: the window becomes an opening in the dome's drum.
- Surprising: it opens up into an oculus at the crown.
- Smallest: the drum steps back so the window keeps its view.
- Verbs: frame, yield.

**24 · a stair placed on a tower, wrapping round it.**
- Obvious: the stair winds round the outside to the top, landings at the windows.
- Surprising: it starts outside and goes in halfway, carrying on inside, coming out on the top.
- Smallest: one straight flight up one face, a door at its head.
- Verbs: wrap, step, open.

## Stage 2: what each delight would take

Asked only now: what geometry, connectivity or knowledge of context each outcome needs.

| Foundation | Encounters needing it | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Yield**: local reshaping round something kept (notch, bow, chamfer, stop short, step back) | 01, 03, 07, 11, 14, 15, 19, 23 | a piece's outline can give way locally, by a cell or part of one, without becoming a different piece |
| **Carry renegotiated**: new supports, or things that weren't supports becoming ones (a frame, a rock, a pier, a buttress) | 02, 06, 07, 10, 16, 17, 18 | support as a relation to whatever's there, not only piers and arches the rules raise |
| **Ports and arrivals**: a gap in a guard, a door, a landing where something arrives | 01, 05, 12, 15, 19, 24 | where a route meets an edge, the edge opens; where it meets a wall, a door |
| **Reserved void**: headroom kept over a flight, a passage through mass | 01, 05, 07, 14, 24 | (built: `World.passages`) |
| **Water with levels and paths**: spouts, falls, a cistern, water through mass | 08, 10, 20, 21 | water held at levels, falling between them along chosen paths, through or past structure |
| **Shelter over a place**: a roof, canopy, dome over a well or stair | 02, 03, 07, 20 | roofs not only over buildings: over places, on whatever carries them |
| **Inside implied**: a window making a room, rooms in a thick wall, a room over an arcade | 04, 13 | buildings recognised from openings and enclosure, not only solid grounded columns |
| **Living things kept**: a tree's trunk and crown as reserved space | 11, 14 | trees as authored things with a claim on space (trunk and crown), like a passage |
| **Shared rhythm**: bays, columns, steps falling into step | 08, 09 | an assembly's order (its bay rhythm) as something a neighbour can join |
| **Terrain merging**: bank, rock and structure blending | 06, 16, 22 | rock and earth as materials structure can rest on, step on, be cut into |
| **Off the grid**: polygonal landings, bowed parapets, chamfers | 11, 19 | edges and corners that aren't only the grid's |

Verbs, by how many of the 24 encounters call on them: **frame** 14, **merge** 11, **step** 10, **yield** 8, **shelter** 7, **carry** 7, **open** 5, **wrap** 4, **remain separate** 1.

## Stage 3: six encounters, a third thing to keep

Each drawn again with something already there that must survive. The question: does the delight survive, and if so, by which of its outcomes?

- **01 (terrace round a stair) + a terrace on top of the joint.** The slot (smallest) leaves the upper terrace unsupported over the flight: it needs a span over the stairwell, and the span must keep the flight's headroom. The covered stair (surprising) becomes the natural answer: the upper terrace *is* its cover. *Delight survives by switching outcome.* Needs: support over a reserved void.
- **03 (houses round a well) + a window on top of the joint.** The chamfered corner (smallest) would cut through the window. Either the chamfer goes to the other side of the corner, or the window becomes the opening of the loggia over the well (surprising). *Survives by switching outcome.* Needs: openings as authored, kept through reshaping.
- **05 (tower at a court's end) + a balcony a cell to the side.** The tower should give on to the balcony: a door from the tower to it, so the balcony becomes the tower's lookout. *The delight compounds.* Needs: ports matched across assemblies at height.
- **10 (pool on an arcade) + a thick wall on top of the joint.** The wall divides the pool; water passes through it in a spout: the wall becomes a dam with a fountain. *Compounds.* Needs: water through mass.
- **16 (balcony on rock) + a pergola a cell to the side.** The pergola reaches over the balcony and shades it; its posts stand on the rock, never across the view. *Compounds.* Needs: what a place looks out on (a view) as something to keep clear.
- **17 (buttress refuge on a bridge) + a cypress just behind.** The refuge bay would run into the crown: it moves to the other side of the bridge, or the tree becomes its shade with the bay drawn back from the trunk. *Survives by moving or yielding.* Needs: a tree's crown as reserved space.

In three of six (05, 10, 16) the third thing made the result richer; in the other three (01, 03, 17) the delight survived only because the encounter had another good outcome to switch to.

## What this suggests

1. **Every encounter needs several valid resolutions, not one.** In stage 3 the right answer was often not the encounter's "best" one in isolation but the one compatible with what else was there. The system wants candidates per encounter and a compatibility check (reserved voids, openings, water paths, crowns, views), choosing among them, rather than one rule per pairing.
2. **The three outcomes form a ladder that the hand could climb.** The smallest sufficient (a clean joint) on first contact; the obvious when the same action is repeated or held; the surprising offered as another reading. That makes the restraint Astra asked for the default, and the flourish something the player summons by insisting, which fits what "doing it again" already means here (it develops).
3. **The verbs are more fundamental than the pieces.** *Frame, merge, step, yield, carry* account for most of the delights. Yield and carry need foundations that don't exist yet: local reshaping that keeps a piece's identity, and support as a relation to whatever's there (rock, frame, pier), not only the rules' own piers and arches.
4. **The claims to space grow.** Passages are kept now; this exercise adds openings (windows, doors), a tree's crown, water's path and a place's view. These are the same kind of thing as a reserved void, and could share its mechanism.
5. **New relative to the connective spec:** shelter over places (not only buildings), water as a path through and past structure, living things with claims, and shared rhythm between neighbouring assemblies.

The next step this points to is building the encounter ladder for one pairing that's nearly in reach (a flight meeting a terrace, 15: notch, landing, half-landing), with the compatibility check, before generalising.

## Built: the encounter framework

What the exercise asked of the engine is now its foundation (`client/labyrinth/encounters.ts`), so a new kind of encounter is a small rule and inherits everything else:

- **Rules find meetings and offer a ladder.** A rule says where its meetings are (which of the hand's things meet, where it would act) and, for each, its candidates as ordinary operations, built from shared verbs (`floor`, `pier`, `lantern`, `join`, more to come), at rung 0 (the smallest sufficient), 1 (the obvious) and 2 (the surprising).
- **The framework chooses.** The rung is the hand's choice if it made one (kept on its gesture as `Op.choose`), else the smallest, a rung higher for each later gesture that makes it again (a rule says what counts: for ways meeting, another way, not the stone round it). The highest candidate at or below that rung that keeps every **claim** (`World.claim`: a passage the hand made, a stair's way, a tree, water) and doesn't fall where another meeting's resolution lies is laid.
- **What it lays is just operations.** Marked `derived` (never kept, worked out again on every rebuild), laid after the hand's, so supports, buildings, the walk and the mesh treat it as they treat the hand's. A rebuild is two passes when anything meets.
- **What the hand builds on, it makes its own.** If a gesture stands on stone a meeting laid (a way arriving on a meeting's landing), that stone is copied into the gesture (`World.adopt`), so it no longer depends on how the meeting is read as more is built round it. (Found by the land tests: without it a later way lost its floor when the meeting it rested on was re-read.)
- **The hand can see and choose.** A stroke that makes a meeting says what it became ("…; where it meets: a corner slab joining them") and offers the other rungs when held; selecting a meeting's slab offers its rungs as readings.
- **Every rule is checked the same way** (`devtools/labyrinth-connectors.mjs`, `meetings()`): in a scene turned to all four sides and mirrored, each meeting is found; left alone it's the smallest; each rung can be chosen and is what's laid; nothing laid is in a passage or a stair's way; the things that met can be walked between at every rung; each rung lays all the one below does; and made again, it climbs. These checks found three bugs in the first rule before it shipped (one meeting found twice from each end, a repeat found as a third, the target's own columns counted as insistence).

The first rule, **ways meet** (encounters 12 and 19, and Astra's two bridges meeting diagonally): two raised ways of the hand's ending corner to corner, a cell apart in line, or a way a cell short of a top at its height. Rungs: a corner slab, or the gap closed; a small terrace or landing where they meet; that on its own pier, with a lantern.

### Adding an encounter

1. Write the rule in `encounters.ts`: `find(w)` returns meetings, each `{ rule, name, anchor, cells, by, candidates, insists? }`; candidates are operations made with the verbs (add a verb if a new kind of change is needed, once, for every rule after).
2. Add it to `RULES`.
3. Add a scene to `devtools/labyrinth-connectors.mjs` that makes the encounter by gestures, calling `meetings(w, tag, ruleId)`: all the checks above run, in eight orientations.
4. `--render` draws the scene at every rung in WebGL.

Next rules, by what the exercise found most (frame, merge, step, yield, carry): a flight meeting a terrace (15: notch, top landing, half-landing), two flights opposed (switchback), a way meeting a tree (11, 14: yield, bow, ring), a balcony on rock (16: carry by what's there).
