# The tree, from first principles

The seat revoked the premise this work had been leaning on — *"the frond recipe
the lab approved"* — and asked for a critique and a design from first
principles, in COMPLEMENT to the visual aesthetic of drive's world rather than
merely within it. This is that. Nothing here is built yet; the measurements
are, and they are what the argument rests on.

The revoked premise mattered more than it looks. Every judgement about trees in
this repo, including the closure metric I added last week, has been anchored on
`conifer conic 1-4` being right and the growth forms being wrong. **That anchor
does not survive being looked at at the size the game draws a tree.**

---

## 1. What the medium can hold

Two numbers decide everything and neither was in the argument before now.

**THE FRAME IS 148 x 320 ART PIXELS.** `PIX_H` is 320 rendered rows, the width
follows the aspect, and the result is magnified onto the glass with
NearestFilter. `rtScene.samples = 0` — **there is no MSAA in the game** — and
the composite then quantises to **14 tone levels** and Bayer-dithers at that
resolution.

**A METRE AT DISTANCE d IS 307/d ART PIXELS** (chase fov 55 vertical, 320 rows).
So for a 16 m conifer:

| distance | tree | its crown box | a base pad (2.4 m) | its trunk (0.35 m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 m | 98 px | 98 x 54 | 15 px | 2.2 px |
| 100 m | 49 px | 49 x 27 | 7.4 px | 1.1 px |
| **200 m** | **25 px** | 25 x 13 | 3.7 px | **0.54 px** |
| 400 m | 12 px | 12 x 7 | **1.8 px** | 0.27 px |
| 700 m (rack default) | 7 px | — | 1.0 px | 0.15 px |

A snag's branch is ~0.1 m: **0.3 px at 100 m.** It has been sub-pixel at every
distance it has ever been drawn at.

**THE CONTROL SET HAS BEEN JUDGING TREES AT FOUR TO TEN TIMES THE SIZE THE GAME
DRAWS THEM.** `__ezsheet` renders 224 px cells, with `samples: 4`, and reads the
render target directly — no quantiser, no dither, no nearest magnify. A tree in
a wood is 12 to 50 art pixels tall. So the sheet is a picture of a tree nobody
will ever see, taken through an instrument the renderer does not own. That is
the same fault this file records three times over in another vocabulary — *a
probe that reports the output of a rule cannot witness the rule*, and *measure
the instrument before measuring with it*.

Re-rendered at the true scale (`PX=40` ~ 100-120 m, `PX=20` ~ 200-240 m), the
answer inverts:

- **`conic 1-4` do not read as spruces at 40 px.** They are a blur of
  overlapping horizontal smears with no legible outline; `conic 2` reads as
  scribble. What the lab approved was a 224 px tree.
- **The growth forms read BETTER at 40 px than the base recipe does** — a clear
  trunk and six distinct whorl tiers. At 20 px both are gone, but the growth
  forms are gone into grey stipple and the base into a dark smudge.
- **At 20 px nothing in the atlas reads as a tree except the round broadleaf
  and the palm's solid cone** — and the palm's cone is the cleanest cell on the
  sheet, which is the finding, because it is the one shape that is simply a
  shape.
- **Every crown is one or two tone levels.** There is no sunlit top, no shaded
  underside, no light/dark split at all — the crowns are flat near-black masses
  against the sky.
- **The trunk reads LIGHTER than the crown** past ~100 m, because it is
  sub-pixel and averages with the sky. With the sheet's MSAA that is a grey bar;
  in the game, with no MSAA, it is a 1 px line that flickers on and off as the
  truck moves.

## 2. The critique

### 2.1 The crown is built from features the frame cannot hold

A pad is 1.8 to 7.4 art pixels across over the band where most of a wood is
drawn. With no MSAA and a nearest magnify, a primitive near 1 px does not get
smaller — **it gets INTERMITTENT**, covering a pixel or not depending on
sub-pixel phase, and the phase changes every frame the truck moves. Then the
dither quantises the result. That is a crawling stipple, and it is what the
20 px sheet shows as grey haze.

The doctrine already states the rule this breaks: *prefer binary alpha, flat
stepped tones and hard edges*, and *texture must be coarse and high-contrast to
survive*. A hundred 3 px pads is the definition of fine and low-contrast.

### 2.2 The triangle bill is inverted against the renderer's own economics

Measured off the true-scale sheet: `conic 1` is **1,262 triangles** and covers
**432 art pixels** at 100 m — **2.9 triangles per drawn pixel** — and **10.5 per
pixel** at 200 m. At Yosemite the tree tier is 2.4M triangles, 69% of the whole
frame's geometry, at 22.9 fps.

The doctrine states the economics outright: *per-fragment work is the cheap
resource here; per-vertex and per-draw is the scarce one. Procedural beats
textured.* The crown does the exact opposite — it spends the scarce resource ten
to one on detail the frame cannot resolve, and spends nothing on the fragment.

And the good conifer is good **by accident of overdraw**: 126 pads of 7.4 px
into a 27 x 34 px box is 5.9x coverage. It reads as a mass because it is
effectively solid. The same silhouette is twenty triangles.

### 2.3 Closure is the wrong score, and it flattered the wrong tree

Closure is the share of the bounding box the silhouette fills. It cannot
distinguish a connected mass from the same area of confetti, which is precisely
the difference between a tree that reads and one that does not — and the two
sheets show a 27% cell (`conic 1`) reading worse than a 22% one (`open whorled
6`) at the size that matters.

What actually decides legibility at 12-50 px:

- **connected components** of the silhouette, and the share in the largest —
  a tree is one mass with a trunk, not a cloud of blobs;
- **contrast against the sky and against the ground**, in palette steps — a
  crown one step from its background is not there;
- **internal tone structure**: how many of the 14 levels the crown carries and
  how large the largest same-tone region is. Two or three big regions read as
  form; twenty small ones read as noise, which the dither then amplifies.

### 2.4 Tone is randomised where it should be oriented

`faceTone` spreads tone per FACE by a hash (±0.16) with a foot-in-shadow ramp.
On a 6 px pad that is high-frequency tonal noise at exactly the frequency the
dither works at. On a large form it would be facets — which is on-aesthetic —
but it is keyed on a hash rather than on which way the face points, so it
carries no information about the light. The sheet shows the consequence: crowns
with no lit side.

### 2.5 What was RIGHT and must be kept

Not everything here is wrong, and the parts that are right are the expensive
parts to have got right:

- **the wood.** EZ-Tree's branch skeleton is structurally good, cheap, varied,
  and it is what makes a stand look grown rather than stamped.
- **the form vocabulary** (`round columnar conic umbrella palm bare`), measured
  off the geometry rather than declared — the guild asks for a shape and the
  bake proves it made one.
- **the two-scale palette** — a district grows a few silhouettes, a stand grows
  one. That is what stopped the confetti of species.
- **`aWood`, the sway weights, the bark frame, the instancing and the shadow
  twin.** All of it survives any change to what the crown is made of.
- **the impostor atlas**, which photographs whatever the near tree is. A
  cleaner near tree is a cleaner card for free.

## 3. The design

**A tree in drive is a silhouette, a light/dark split and a trunk. Everything
else is below the quantiser.** Three rules follow, and they are the design.

### RULE 1 — NOTHING IS DRAWN SMALLER THAN THE FRAME CAN HOLD

Every feature of a tree gets a floor in ART PIXELS, and the floor is enforced in
the vertex shader from the instance's own projected size, not by a distance
swap. This is a rule this codebase already has in three places: the chart's
ribbon (`max(hw x base, 0.5)` pixels of ground), the substrate's `tdBand`, and
the impostor's fragment-side fade.

- **the trunk** never goes under ~1.2 px, and when it is widened it is DARKENED
  toward the crown's tone so it does not average to sky — a widened pale bar is
  the white-contour-diagram fault the doctrine forbids;
- **a snag's branches** take the same floor, or a snag past ~120 m is a card and
  admits it;
- **the crown's own lobes** take the floor by MERGING (rule 2), not by growing.

### RULE 2 — THE CROWN IS A FEW LARGE MASSES THAT CLOSE WITH DISTANCE

Replace the 30-126 pads with **6 to 14 boughs**, each a low-poly mass of 6-12
triangles, placed on the skeleton's outer branch tips, sized so neighbours
overlap by roughly a third. At 40 px a bough is 8-14 px — a feature the frame
can hold. Crown cost: **60-150 triangles against 470-1,300.**

Then the mechanism that makes it work at every distance, and it costs three
lines and no geometry: **each crown vertex carries its bough's centre, and the
vertex shader collapses the boughs toward a common envelope as the tree's
projected height falls.**

```
p = mix(p_bough, envelope(dir(p - crownCentre)), t),   t = f(projected px)
```

Near, the boughs are distinct with sky between them and the silhouette is
ragged at the bough scale. Far, they merge into one solid envelope with a hard
outline — **the stipple cannot happen, because by the time a bough would be 1 px
there are no boughs.** It is an LOD with no second geometry, no popping (t is
continuous) and no re-upload.

### RULE 3 — THREE TONES, BY ORIENTATION, DELIBERATELY STEPPED

The crown's shading is quantised IN THE MATERIAL to ~3 levels aligned with the
palette's own step, split by the bough's outward normal and its depth into the
crown: **sunlit outer, body, shaded under-inner**. `faceTone`'s hash stays, at a
fraction of its current spread, as per-bough variation rather than as the tone
itself.

This is the half the sheets say is missing entirely, and it is the half that
makes a 25 px tree read as a solid object rather than a hole in the sky.

### …and the silhouette raggedness is authored per form

Not inherited from whatever the reduction left behind:

| form | the mass | the ragged edge |
|---|---|---|
| conic | 5-7 whorl tiers, widening downward | tier tips, ~1/6 of crown height apart |
| round | 5-8 lobes on a domed envelope | lobe rim, ~1/3 of crown width |
| columnar | a narrow mass, vertical lobing | shallow, at ~1/5 of height |
| umbrella | a flat plate, thicker at the centre | lobed rim only |
| **palm** | **6-10 long thin fronds, drooping** | the fronds ARE the silhouette |
| bare | no crown; the wood, at the pixel floor | — |

The palm is the one form that must not be a mass at all, and it is currently a
solid cone. `branch.force` is what makes a palm a palm; the bake is not using
it. It is also the form the sheets say reads BEST, because a solid shape beats
a cloud — so the fix is fronds at the pixel floor, not more cone.

## 4. What this buys, and what it costs

**Triangles.** ~100-200 a tree against 470-1,300. At Yosemite's 2,300 drawn
trees that is **0.3M rather than 2.4M** — the tree tier stops being 69% of the
frame's geometry. Which is the seat's own question from the impostor work,
answered: the headroom for fuller, more realistic trees is not more triangles,
it is the ten to one the current crown is wasting on sub-pixel detail.

**Risk, stated.** The vertex-side merge has to be shown not to pop and not to
break the shadow twin (the shadow pass runs the same vertex shader, so it should
follow for free, but that is a claim to test). The per-bough attribute is 3
floats a vertex on the crown — real, and small against the triangles removed.
And a re-bake changes every variant at once, so the control set has to be able
to say whether it improved anything, which is why the instrument comes first.

**The alternative I am NOT recommending**, so it is on the record: an envelope
with a procedural alpha-cut foliage boundary in the fragment (discard on a 3D
field, band-limited by `fwidth`). It is the purest expression of the doctrine —
binary alpha, hard edges, procedural, per-fragment — and it would be ~40-80
triangles a tree. It is not the recommendation because `discard` breaks early-Z
on the tile GPUs this ships to, it needs a second custom depth material for the
shadow pass, and the band limit has to be tuned before anything can be judged.
Rule 2 gets most of the win with none of that, and rule 2 does not preclude it
later: an envelope that already exists is the surface a cut would be applied to.

## 5. The order to do it in

**PHASE 0 — THE INSTRUMENT, AND IT IS NOT OPTIONAL.** Every judgement above
rests on rendering at the true art scale; the current sheet cannot do it
honestly because of its MSAA and because it skips the post chain. So:
`__ezsheet` renders at `samples: 0` and runs the composite's own quantise and
Bayer over the cell before reading it, at PX derived from a stated DISTANCE
rather than a pixel count; and the score becomes components, largest-component
share, sky contrast in palette steps, and distinct tone levels — with closure
kept beside them as one column of four rather than as the number. Retire
closure as the headline. **Nothing else can be evaluated until this exists.**

**PHASE 1 — re-photograph the atlas as it stands, through phase 0**, at 100 m,
200 m and 400 m, and record it as the real baseline. The numbers in this
document are a first reading through a flawed instrument and should not be
quoted once a better one exists.

**PHASE 2 — rule 3 alone**, on the current geometry: orientation-driven, stepped
tone. It is a material change, it touches no bake, and it is the cheapest thing
on this list that the 40 px sheet says is missing.

**PHASE 3 — rules 1 and 2**: the bough crown and the distance merge, one form at
a time, conifer first because it is the family the seat reported and the family
the district palettes at Yosemite actually draw.

**PHASE 4 — the palm's fronds, and the snag's pixel floor.** Both are forms
that are currently drawing the wrong thing rather than drawing it badly.

**PHASE 5 — re-bake the whole atlas against phase 1's baseline**, and re-run the
impostor atlas, which gets the improvement for nothing.
