# stillwater — review of the first cut (deploy 1790589062515)

The cell as pulled (`cells/stillwater/index.ts`, tree `f37f1ff6d1`) is a single
14.6 KB line: an HTML page inside a JS string literal, served by a one-line
handler. This review is of that page, measured against the screenshot from the
phone and the two reference frames it was aiming at.

## What works

- **The palette and restraint.** Deep teal water, olive-to-lime leaves, a warm
  serif title, no score, no failure screen. The mood is right and worth keeping.
- **Gentle failure.** Overshooting lets the drops "return to the water" after
  1.4 s rather than flashing an error. Tapping water clears. Tracing across pads
  selects them, back-tracing pops. These are good, child-safe rules.
- **Accessibility intent.** A live region announces "Gather n drops. k gathered",
  the canvas is focusable, Escape clears, 1–5 pick pads, reduced motion is read.
- **Sound off by default** with a lazily created `AudioContext`.

## What holds it back

### It is a sequence of screens, not a place

- Every round lays out **the same five pads at the same five fixed screen
  positions** (`W*(.5+[-.14,.16,-.13,.14,0][i])`, `H*(.24+i*.088)`), a zig-zag
  column down the middle. Pad values come from a **four-entry table**
  (`[[1,2,3,2,2],…][round%4]`) and targets from a **seven-entry cycle**
  (`[5,6,7,8,6,9,10]`), so the whole game repeats within a minute.
- **The boat never moves.** It is pinned at `H*.76`; "travel" only slides the
  bank sprites down by `H*.38` and wraps them (`if(p.y>H+230)p.y=-230`), so the
  same 38 bank leaves recycle forever. Then `layoutPads()` hard-cuts the next
  five pads into place. That cut is exactly the "progression between fixed
  screens" feeling.
- **Nothing touches anything.** No physics: pads don't drift, collide, or get
  pushed by the boat. The rope is a fixed bezier to the bottom of the screen.

### Interactive pads are a separate species

- The five answer pads are uniformly small, isolated in open water, and carry
  drops in a tidy arc with a radial glow — they read as **buttons laid on top of
  a background**, which is what they are. The banks are 12 pre-rendered sprites
  at 19 % or 94 % opacity and are inert.

### Rendering is flat and expensive at the same time

- Canvas 2D. Lighting is one radial gradient and a vignette; there is no sun,
  so leaves have no consistent light direction (each sprite's gradient is drawn
  after a random rotation, so the "lit side" differs per leaf).
- Leaves are 256 px sprites scaled up to ~200 device px on a 2× phone — the
  bank leaves in the screenshot are visibly soft.
- Per frame it allocates a `createPattern`, two full-screen gradients, **one
  radial gradient per dew drop**, and draws ~40 leaves with `shadowBlur` — the
  most expensive thing canvas 2D can do on mobile Safari.
- Depth is suggested only by the 19 %-opacity "deep" leaves. Fish are nine
  translucent ellipses swimming straight up at constant speed.

### The dew drops are lights, not water

- Each drop is a 2.8 px disc + a 1 px highlight + an 11 px glow. On the phone
  they read as fairy lights / LEDs. Real dew is a lens: it magnifies the leaf
  beneath, has a dark refracted rim, a pinpoint specular, a bright caustic on the
  shadow side and a soft contact shadow.

### Numeracy stops at addition

- Only subset-sum addition to 5–10. There is no progression toward grouping or
  multiplication, and the fixed tables mean solvability is guaranteed by
  construction rather than by the rules.

### Code shape

- All logic lives in a JS string inside `index.ts`: not type-checked, not
  diffable, not testable, against the workspace tenets (TypeScript front and
  back, composable pieces). `window.__stillwater` is rebuilt every frame.

## Direction taken in the rebuild

Following the feedback — *an immersive, endless landscape; high-detail shaders;
flocking fish; real depth and lighting; the boat gently bumping pads that float
and interact; every pad the same; one lush, meditative journey propelled by the
same numeracy rules from addition through to multiplication; hyper-real dew* —
the cell is rebuilt as a WebGL2 client (`client/`, bundled to `app.js` by the
platform) with a small static shell:

| Concern | Now |
| --- | --- |
| World | Endless procedural river: chunks of pads generated ahead along a meandering channel, recycled behind. No rounds, no layouts. |
| Motion | The boat really travels; the camera follows. Solving gives a glide proportional to the target; between solves it drifts. |
| Physics | Every pad is a body tethered by a slack stem: drag, soft pad–pad contact with spin, and a capsule boat that shoulders pads aside (with a soft bump sound and ripple). |
| Water | GPU wave simulation (taps, boat bow, oar strokes, bumped pads) drives refraction and glints; riverbed with depth-varying absorption, animated caustics and pad shadows cast through the water column. |
| Depth | Three layers under the surface — bed, fish at varying depth (tinted, softened, shadowed), submerged leaves — seen through a refracting surface. |
| Fish | Boids: separation/alignment/cohesion within depth bands, flee the boat and taps, undulating procedural bodies. |
| Pads | One procedural shader for every pad: notch, scalloped rim, raised veins, curled edges, waxy specular under one sun. |
| Dew | Per-drop lens in the pad shader: magnified refraction of the leaf, dark refracted rim, fresnel sky, pinpoint specular, caustic focus and contact shadow. |
| Numeracy | Stages: gather (≤5) → add (≤10) → add more (≤20, ten-frames) → equal groups (array display) → times (numeral). Targets are drawn from the pads actually in view; if drift makes a target unsolvable, dew condenses on an empty pad in view to restore a solution. `client/numeracy.ts` is pure and unit-tested. |

## Round 2 — riverbed, depth, currents, atmosphere, touch as wind

Feedback: *focus on the river bed and depth/currents, fix detail and realism;
fireflies and atmosphere; touch like a general but firm breeze, rippling the
water and disturbing pads; and "this pond needs WebGL2" on iOS Safari.*

**iOS.** That message was printed for *any* renderer throw. Field reports
(`client/report.ts` → `GET /report` → `cells.logs`) from iOS 18.7 / Safari 26
on an Apple GPU showed WebGL2 and float buffers fully supported and every
shader compiling in isolation (a per-program probe in fresh contexts) — but the
main context was already lost before the first compile. Dropping
`powerPreference: 'high-performance'` and asking for `EXT_color_buffer_float`
only when the wave targets are built fixed it (first attempt boots). The
renderer still checks for loss after each setup step and main.ts retries on a
fresh canvas with plainer attributes, reporting which attempt held.

**Depth.** The bed is drawn with parallax (`DEPTH_K`): deeper things look
smaller and slide slower under the surface as the boat moves; fish, weeds,
sunken leaves and suspended silt each sit at their own depth, fireflies above
the water slide faster. Water is clearer (per-channel absorption, red first).

**Bed.** Silt in the slack, pale sand where the current scours, ripple marks
across the flow lit on their upstream face, gravel beds of lit stones with
algae crowns and contact shadows, sunken twigs, leaf litter, caustics drifting
downstream and sharper in the shallows, pad shadows now cast *away* from the
sun (the first cut had the sign backwards).

**Current.** One flow field — along the channel, fastest in the run, eddying
where it meets the slack — drives a flow-mapped surface, pads leaning on their
stems, fish holding station nose-upstream, weeds streaming, silt and pollen.

**Atmosphere.** A 15-minute day: afternoon → golden hour → dusk → moonlit →
dawn, with mist, fireflies (blinking, with their light on the water), and the
bow lantern as a real point light on pads, bed and ripples.

**Touch.** Open water *or a dry leaf* is wind: a tap is a round puff, a drag a
firm gust that follows the finger and runs on across the water — roughened,
darkened, glinting water, pads pushed and turned, fish and fireflies
scattered, a breeze in the sound. Leaves holding dew are still the choices.

## Round 3 — the boat is yours, wind on water, schools

*Touch/drag on the boat should row; waiting for a solve broke the endless
journey. Wind should raise ripples and wakes (the boat too). Smaller fish in
bigger schools, reacting to touch.*

- **The boat never waits.** It rows on at a steady cruise; a solve adds a
  surge. A finger on the boat takes the helm — it turns toward the finger and
  rows harder the further ahead you pull; a tap on it is one long stroke.
  After letting go it holds that course a few seconds, then finds the channel.
- **Wakes.** A V off the bow and quarters, a trail off the stern and a
  puddle at each oar catch, all into the wave simulation; pads sliding through
  the water push their own little waves. The sim now takes 48 impulses a step
  (sampled evenly when busy) and the surface shows them more strongly.
- **Wind on water.** A gust drives a front of crests along its leading edge
  (a puff, an expanding ring) plus cat's-paw ripples inside it.
- **Schools.** Four schools of ~22 minnows flock only with their own school,
  tightly, turn as one and flash silver on the turn; a dozen larger fish
  cruise among them. Taps, gusts (continuously as they pass) and hull bumps
  scare them; a school bolts together and is re-seeded whole if left behind.

## Round 4 — fireflies, soft pads, lantern dew, the plop, the wake

- **Fireflies** are small and busy: they dart and hang in quick changes of
  heading, and flash briefly (some twice) instead of swelling slowly.
- **Pads don't glow.** A chosen leaf shows only through its dew: the drops
  light warm from within and spill a little light onto the leaf around them.
- **Soft pads.** Each pad carries a press vector (toward what it meets —
  neighbours or the hull — with length = how far its edge gives) and a flex.
  The shader flattens the pressed arc into a chord and lifts it (it rides up),
  and a knock sets the leaf wobbling along the knock, dying away.
- **Lantern in the dew.** Every drop within reach carries the lantern's own
  pinpoint and a warm focus beneath it, well beyond the pool of light.
- **The tap plop.** The "spiral star" was the puff's ring of impulses spun by
  time; a tap is now one impulse — one clean ring — and the puff only pushes.
- **The wake.** The wave sim's ripples outrun a rowing boat, so they can only
  make rings; the V is drawn from the boat's recent path — two lit crests with
  troughs spreading at the Kelvin angle, and churned white water off the
  stern — on top of the sim's ripples and a new foam channel (oars, bumps).
- Fixed a blank frame whenever the adaptive resolution stepped (the resize ran
  after the draw).

## Round 5 — oar water that travels

The oars used to make one ripple at the catch. Now the stroke cycle moves
water the way a real one does: a splash at the catch; while the blade drives
aft it drags a line of disturbance and sheds packets of pushed water that
travel aft (the direction the stroke threw them) and a little outward,
stirring the surface and slowing to rest; at the release, the pair of swirls
either side of the blade. The blade reads wet exactly while it drives.

## Round 6 — stepping back: everything that moves the water is felt by what floats on it

The feedback — oars rowing over the leaves, no drops off the blades — named a
wider gap: each element answered the touch, but not each other. The rule now
is the matrix below; anything new should find its row.

| cause | water | pads | fish | sound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tap on water | one plop, ring | ring front lifts, pushes, flexes them | bolt | — |
| tap on a leaf / dry leaf | — (the leaf takes it) | dips, shoved, flexes | — | — |
| drag (wind) | crest front, cat's paws, darker glinting patch | pushed, turned, flutter | bolt as it passes | breeze |
| oar catch | splash + foam, ring | on a leaf: leaf dunked & shoved instead | bolt | plunk |
| oar drive | trail + packets thrown aft | blade shoulders leaves aside and aft | — | — |
| oar release | pair of swirls, rings | as catch | — | — |
| oar recovery | drips: glint → tiny ring | drip on a leaf nudges it | — | ticks |
| hull | bow V, quarter waves, stern wash, rings on bumps | pressed, pushed, turned, flattened edge | shy | knock |
| current | flow-mapped surface | lean on stems | face upstream | — |
| lantern | glow + glints | warms leaves | — | — |

`Pond.splash` is the one door for "something meets the water": on open water
it makes the ripple and a ring the pads will feel (`rings`, travelling at the
sim's wave speed); on a leaf it moves the leaf instead. Dew shivers as its
leaf flexes.

## Round 7 — leaves, not frying pans; a pond, not just pads

**Why they read as pans:** a raised rim all the way round (`h += curl *
smoothstep(.78, 1, len)`), a dome, a near-perfect circle, straight spoke veins
and a contact-shadow ring that read as wall thickness.

**Now:** flat on the water with a slow drape and a small sag at the stem; the
margin lifts only in a few stretches (`edgeLift`, breathing slowly), which is
also the only place the red underside and a real shadow show; the outline is
slightly oval with two- and three-lobe irregularity, fine scallops, a narrow
off-axis sinus with rounded lobe tips, and the odd insect nibble; veins are
fainter and sweep in a curve; gloss is patchy (wet film vs dull); a fine pale
margin runs along the edge.

**Other things afloat** (`Floater`, drawn beneath the pads' edges): duckweed
rafts gathered in the slack water with a few loose fronds; petals shed around
the lilies; the odd fallen willow leaf; closed lily buds among the bank pads.
They drift with the current, blow with the wind, part around the hull, are
swept aft by the blades and rock outward on every ripple ring — they join the
interaction matrix under "pads" wherever pads react.

## Round 8 — the oars are the engine

Speed used to be set directly and the oars merely animated (faster boat =
faster flailing). Now thrust comes only from blades in the water: the cadence
is calm and nearly constant (~2.3–2.7 s a stroke), and `power` — how hard each
stroke pulls — is the one control. The boat surges on each drive and glides,
slowing, between strokes (light cruise ≈ 10–16 u/s through a stroke, full
power ≈ 32–56). A harder pull is a longer stroke (the blades reach further
and sweep further back), a deeper lean, bigger puddles and more foam. The helm
sets power by how far ahead you pull; a tap or a solved number spends `surge`
on full-power strokes; turning is only as quick as the boat is moving.

## Round 9 — the blade and the leaf

**The oar turns.** The blade feathers flat on the return (wide from above,
lifted — its shadow falls further) and squares on edge through the pull (thin
from above); through the pull its end is under the water, seen dimmer and
green through the river with a waterline where it goes in, and casts no
shadow there.

**A blade that lands on a leaf catches it.** It no longer hops out of the way.
The struck edge is pushed *under* (`Pad.sink`): the shader floods that side —
darker, cooler, flatter, light playing on the water sheeting over it, a
wavering waterline — and the leaf pivots, its far edge tipping up. The leaf is
held by the blade and moves with it (the blade is locked in the water while
the boat passes it, so the leaf is drawn back along the boat); water pours
over the flooded edge; dew that goes under washes off (not on a chosen leaf).
Let go, the leaf comes back up, shedding the water in a ripple. A tap on a
leaf likewise presses it down rather than making it jump.

`?sinktest=1` holds the centre-most dewy leaf pushed under (with a pressed
edge), so the flooding can be looked at without waiting for a blade to land.
`tests/stillwater-oar.test.ts` pins the physics: a caught leaf sinks, is held
(not flung), loses the dew that went under and resurfaces; a tapped leaf
sinks rather than hops.

## Round 10 — what's overhead: sky and canopy, mirrored and cast

One canopy field (`CANOPY` in shaders.ts: trees reaching out from both banks,
an open ribbon of sky over the run, leaves stirring) is used twice, so the two
agree:

- **Mirrored.** The surface reflects it at a virtual depth as far below as the
  trees are above, so the reflection is smaller and slides slower than the
  surface as the boat moves, and ripples, wakes and gusts break it up. Sky
  carries drifting clouds, and stars in the gaps at night. A dark river
  mirrors what's bright above it and turns to a window where the trees are
  dark — you see the bed there.
- **Cast.** `sunThrough` dapples the sun (or moon) on the water, the bed, the
  pads, the floaters and the boat, with flecks through the gaps; sun glints on
  the water only appear where sunlight reaches it.

## Round 11 — the waterline, and the whole plant

**Waterline.** It was a chord with a wobble. It is now where the river's level
meets the leaf's surface: bowed across the pressed side (the leaf bends as it
goes under), wandering with the leaf's drape, running ahead in soft fingers
down the vein grooves, lapping with the little waves, with puddles stranded
just past the line.

**Plants, not discs.** A `Plant` is a rhizome on the bed (knobbly arms in the
mud); every pad belongs to the nearest plant (`plantFor`) and rises on a stem
from it. Stems are drawn under the water with depth and parallax, bowing
downstream with the current and leaning when their leaf is pushed — so an oar
or the hull visibly drags a leaf against its stem. Young leaves unfurl under
the surface on their own short stems. Flowers and buds (`Bloom`) no longer sit
on a pad: they stand among the leaves on their own stems from the same
rhizome, tethered, rocked by ripples, pushed by the hull and oars, and petals
shed around the open ones. Stems of every leaf in view are one batched draw.

## Round 12 — the caustics came back

Adding the canopy (Round 10) had quietly taken the bed's caustics: A/B renders
showed them gone with `sunThrough` on and back with it forced to 1. The shade
lookup threw the canopy's shadow ~190 units toward the sun (a tall-tree
height), which landed the bank trees' shade across the whole open run, and the
canopy also reached too far over mid-river. The leaves are low over the water
now (a ~55 unit throw), the run is open sky, and the sky's veil on the
surface is thinner — caustics dance on the bed again while the banks keep
their dappled shade.

## Round 13 — rope, sound, fish from above

**Rope.** Now a floating line dragged slowly through water: each node moves
relative to the *water* (the current carries it), with water drag split along
and across the rope — sideways motion is damped ~8× harder than sliding along
it — so it streams behind in long lazy curves. It has bending stiffness (no
sharp folds; it leaves the stern cleat pointing aft), drapes round the edges
of leaves (nudging them), pushes through duckweed, and ruffles the water where
it's dragged sideways. Measured: sharpest joint ≤ ~7° among the pads (was up
to 129°), ~35° off the cleat in a hard turn (was 145°); rope nodes inside a
leaf ≈ 0.15 of 28 per sample, never deep.

**Sound.** Replaced by a bubble synthesiser on the audio thread
(AudioWorklet, van den Doel style): water is mostly bubbles ringing at a
size-set pitch that rises as they decay. River = sparse small bubbles + low
lapping; motion and wind raise the rate; drip = one small bubble; tap = one
large bubble + splash; oar catch = splash burst + bubble cluster; release =
gurgle; hull knock = three damped wooden modes + click; wind = breath +
leaf-rustle flutter. Events pan by screen position; a short dark synthetic
reverb gives the air. Falls back to the old node voices without AudioWorklet.
The worklet was exercised in Node (no NaN, no clipping, levels balanced; the
drip's pitch rises).

**Fish from above.** The forked tail fan was a side view. Seen from above the
tail and dorsal are vertical, i.e. edge-on: thin lines. Fish now show a plump
back (widest at the shoulders, slim tail stalk), a dark spine ridge shading to
lighter flanks, eyes bulging at the sides of the head, pectoral and pelvic
fins spread flat, the tail only widening a touch as it sweeps.

## Round 14 — the rower

A small figure in a red riding-hood cloak (storybook, not ominous), facing the
bow — the oars are pushed, the handles are ahead of them. From above: a
rounded hood with a soft point falling back, a shadowed opening with a fringe
of hair and a cheek catching the light; the cape spread over the shoulders
and down the back in soft folds to a rippled hem; sleeved arms reaching to
small hands on the oar handles; the figure leans into each stroke and casts
its shadow with the boat. A wicker basket under a checked cloth rides on the
forward seat. The lantern now lights the whole boat at night, not only the
cloak (the hull's light had left it out).

Field check: every iPhone load since Round 10 booted first time and held
16.7 ms frames (0.8 render scale, wave sim on); no errors reported.

## Round 15 — the oar dip was a snare

It was bright broadband noise with an instant attack and a fast decay — the
recipe for a snare drum. An oar catch is now: a low rising "bloop" (the air
the blade drags under, collapsing: ~140→300 Hz, soft 12 ms onset), a dark
swell of displaced water (35 ms attack), spray as ~26 separate droplet tics
thinning out over a quarter second, and a cluster of bubbles rising after.
Measured against the old one: energy in the first 5 ms fell from 83 % of peak
to 2 % (no crack), and low-band to high-band energy rose from 1.4 to 16 (body,
not hiss), at the same loudness. The tap plop's small splash also swells now
instead of cracking.

## Round 16 — the rower, properly

- **Facing.** The figure was set up for push-rowing: facing the bow, in front of the handles. In a skiff with oars on thole pins you pull, so the rower now sits on the forward thwart (y≈16) facing the **stern**. The hood opening, hair and cheek point aft, and the hands meet the handles where they travel (y≈0.6 at the catch to y≈12.6 at the finish).
- **Swing.** The body now slides along the boat with the stroke: `16 + sin(stroke·2π)·4.5·power`. It reaches toward the stern at the catch and lies back toward the bow at the finish. The head travels a further 40% so the lean reads, and a harder pull swings further.
- **Cloak.** Soft grey wool (hood a shade darker) replaces the red. Felt, grain and fibre come from the noise texture, and the cape, hood and arm edges are fuzzed so the silhouette looks woollen rather than vector-crisp. The picnic basket moved to the stern thwart.

## Round 17 — boat light, the rope's end, a quieter waterline

- **Boat and rower light.** They were lit at full ambient + 0.8–0.9 sun + 1.1× lantern, brighter than anything around them. Now: 0.85 ambient, 0.55 sun, 0.4 lantern (the lantern hangs over the bow behind glass, so the boat gets a warm wash rather than glare). The wood and wool base colours are also a step darker.
- **Rope.** It enables no mechanic. It is a painter line, there to make the current, the boat's turns and the wake legible, and it drapes round and nudges leaves. It used to run 270 units and fade out in its last third (the ribbon helper fades numeric-alpha ribbons), so it looked fixed somewhere off screen. It is now 135 units, solid to the end, with a whipped knot and a small cork float (red tip) that drags a little harder than the line and keeps it streaming.
- **Pad waterline.** Wider, gentler transition (−.04….12), a paler film that deepens less, and a meniscus sheen at a third of the old strength and wider. It reads as a film of water, not a drawn line.

## Round 18 — slow leaves, a soft ribbon, real flowers

- **Pads resurface slowly.** Recovery was `exp(-2.2t)` (half-way in ~0.3 s). It is now `exp(-(0.75 + 0.5·(1−sink))t)`: the water sheeted over the leaf has to run off first, so it lifts slowly from deep and a little quicker near the top. Simulated: half-way in 0.8 s, back to 10% in 2.2 s.
- **Rope → a floating ribbon.** It was stiff: smoothing 0.22 over 6 iterations and 9-unit links. Now it has 26 links of 5.4, stiffness 0.035, softer side drag, and a lighter push on the leaves. It also moves with the surface: the breeze acts on it, and a slow swell and eddies grow toward the free end. Headless, it wanders 8–50 units off the boat's line with bends of 21° or less and no creases. It is drawn as a flat cotton ribbon that twists (narrowing where it turns on edge), with a faint pale meniscus beside it.
- **Flower shimmer.** The stamen speckle came from a `hash(floor(p·60))`, about four cells per pixel, so it crawled as the flower turned, and the petal edges and layer boundaries weren't antialiased. The flower is now built from screen-derivative AA edges and nothing is finer than a pixel.
- **Flower diversity (form, not only colour).** There are six kinds, each with seeded per-petal length, width and lean:
  - white water lily (two rings of pointed petals over green sepals);
  - pink lily (flushed base);
  - yellow lily (a half-open cup of narrow upright petals showing their greener backs);
  - double rose lily (four packed rings);
  - spatterdock (a small yellow globe of five thick sepals round a ribbed stigma disc);
  - a spent flower (slack, browning, petals missing, green seed head).

  Weighted mix: 30/18/12/12/18/10%.


## Round 19 — a still hull, calm fish, a hand at the stern

- **Boat "vibrating".** The wake was a pulsed point source: fixed-strength impulses stamped 30×/s at the stem, shoulders and quarters, whatever the speed. A slow boat therefore rang concentric rings out around itself, and ahead of the bow, since the ripples outran it. Now the hull disturbs the water in proportion to how far it moved that frame (every frame, strength `speed·dt·(0.4 + 1.4k)`). A stationary boat radiates nothing, a slow one a whisper, and the V builds with speed.
- **Fish tails vibrating.** The beat was `uTime × (2.6 + speed·0.12)`. With `uTime` in the hundreds of seconds, any wobble in speed made the phase jump. The phase is now integrated per fish on the CPU at `2.2 + 0.11·effort` rad/s (smaller fish faster), where effort is speed *through the water*, smoothed. A fish holding against the current swishes steadily and gently, and a dart is a flurry. Heading now follows the water-relative swim direction with a smoothed turn, not the ground velocity (near zero when holding station, so it jittered). Markings take a fixed per-fish seed (they used to ride the phase).
- **Input zones.** The boat and the water astern of it are the helm, like a hand at the stern. Resting there keeps a steady stroke, pushing up pulls harder (to full at ~140 px), sliding across steers (~0.4 rad per 60 px), and a tap is one long pull. Dewy leaves still gather anywhere (even astern). Everything ahead is splash and wind, so the finger never covers the water the boat is heading into. Verified in Chromium: holding sets the helm, pushing up raised power 0.22 → 0.84 and turned the boat, and touch ahead doesn't take the helm.

## Round 20 — the pond learns, and resolution can recover

The hidden five-level ladder is gone. A single local proficiency value now
continuously biases an overlapping field of counting, addition, larger
composition, equal groups and bare products. Earlier forms remain in the mix at
high proficiency; later forms emerge smoothly rather than unlocking on a solve
count. Target selection first asks what the dew already present in a stable
stretch ahead can express, then samples the capability field. Dew generation is
also proficiency-shaped independently of the current ask.

A solve updates proficiency with a small difficulty-weighted observation and
the amount of gentle correction on the way. There is still no failure state.
The old v2 ladder migrates once into an approximate continuous starting point;
v3 stores only mastery + total solves. ?stage remains a diagnostic force and
?level=0..1 probes the curriculum field.

Targets now come from a safer future-facing window. If physics later steals an
untouched answer, the ask quietly dissolves and is redrawn from what the
landscape contains. Exact condensation repair is reserved for the rare case
where the child has already started an answer.

Performance: fish neighbour search is spatially bucketed instead of O(n²);
dew refraction reuses leaf material instead of reconstructing a second full
procedural normal per droplet pixel; edge curl and several full-screen water
effects use texture channels to cut fetches. Adaptive resolution can also
recover: the old upscale gate required <13 ms frames, impossible at a 60 Hz
presentation cadence (~16.7 ms), so a temporary down-step became permanent.
Recovery now uses a reachable 18.2 ms threshold with hysteresis.

## Round 21 — the fish come back

Fish face upstream, so they fall behind a boat heading downstream. A keep-near-the-view pull then held them in a band just off-screen, closer than the recycle threshold (300 units behind, 450 to the side), so they were never brought back. Headless over four minutes, all 100 were behind the view after 60 s. Now anything just out of sight (70 behind, 160 to the side) is re-seeded ahead, out of sight, and 23–97 of 100 stay in view throughout. The learning proposal is in `LEARNING-DESIGN.md`.

## Round 22 — the learning arc (LEARNING-DESIGN.md, built)

A new pure module, `client/learning.ts`, with 16 tests in `tests/stillwater-learning.test.ts`.

**P1 — the river remembers.**
- Each answer is a fact (`s:3+4`, `g:3x4`). The two orientations of a product are one fact, and the ways it has been made are remembered.
- Recall decays with a per-fact half-life. A clean answer multiplies it by `1 + q·(0.6 + 2.2·(1−p))`, so a nearly-forgotten fact gains most (the spacing effect). A struggle halves it.
- A fact is due below 75% recall, never within 45 s of being seen, and only if it sits in the child's current band.
- Stored in `stillwater.facts.v1`: the 400 most recent facts. `?fresh=1` ignores the store.

**Planting.** The most urgent due fact's parts are fed into the dew of leaves growing ahead (60% of dewy leaves take the next part), so the river can ask for it when they drift into view. Reach asks (and a returning child's warm-up) look first for a due fact the visible dew can make, skipping the number just asked.

**P2 — silent fluency.** Each answer is scored for quality from:
- seconds against an expected time (finding leaves, plus time per drop when counting);
- friction;
- help given.

Quality drives the fact's half-life. Proficiency now learns from friction plus help. Nothing is shown.

**P3 — stretches.** A stretch runs warm → reach → consolidate → relief ×2 → finale.
- Each phase shifts the proficiency field: −0.12, +0.08, 0, −0.22.
- The pause after an answer depends on the phase; relief comes quick.
- A smooth stretch (≥ 80% first-try) reaches twice next time, and a struggled reach adds a third relief.
- Consolidate asks the reach's number again: "another way" for sums, "the other way round" for products.

**P4 — elegance noticed.**
- The fewest possible leaves (≤ 2, ≥ 5) opens a lily beside one of them: a bud rises and opens.
- A product made the other way round opens twin lilies.
- A double draws the two leaves together.
- Passing exactly through ten on the way to more rings one low bell.
- A fluent answer brightens the lantern.

**P5 — bond asks.** In the add range, 40% of reaches (60% when a due fact is being reviewed) are "n · in two leaves". A second leaf that doesn't make it restarts the pair from the new one. If the pair drifts away, the ask quietly relaxes.

**P6 — finale.** Up to four dewy leaves in view, left to right, chime their count (one rising note per drop) as each drop lifts to the lantern. The stretch's anonymous summary goes to `/report` as `learn`.

**P7 — help without words.** With no touch for 7 s, the leaves of an answer bob and glint. At 14 s, one of them drifts toward the boat. At 22 s they glint again. Help lowers the answer's quality.

**P8 — dice dew.** Patterns for 1–7 drops (subitizing, 6 as 3 and 3), rotated clear of the notch. The chance runs from 0.85 early to 0.12, falling as proficiency grows.

**P9 — welcome back.** After more than 6 h away, the warm-up is two asks (due reviews first) and flowers bloom about twice as often for the first 90 s. There are no streaks.

**Verified in Chromium** with an autoplay through the debug surface (`__stillwater.learning`):
- the full arc cycles, a bond is asked and answered as a pair, and "another way" follows a reach;
- with history seeded as "yesterday", the first asks are due facts, and `3+4` grew from 900 s to about 9,600 s after clean recalls;
- hesitation triggers help;
- a two-leaf answer opens a lily at the leaf.

**Not yet:**
- the "boat has caught a leaf" missing-part ask and the make-ten stretch (P5);
- the surplus drops sliding back one by one on "over";
- the rope abacus.

## Round 25 — row through, don't bounce around

Hull contact now drives continuous pad load/submergence. The previous hull
response never set the leaf's active-load state and instead applied large
sideways impulse + positional correction, which is why the boat bounced around
pads while only oars could sink them. Pads now flood under sustained hull
pressure, progressively lose collision authority, overlap the hull when below
draft, then resurface under buoyancy after release. A small side shoulder,
heading deflection and speed loss remain while the leaf is afloat.

Piers are now generated from explicit water-tip and land-root endpoints; roots
sit deep in bank vegetation or clip beyond the grown field/off-screen, eliminating
detached floating pier geometry.

## Round 40 — reaches: the river changes as you travel it

- **Channel openness:** the half width scales by `0.55 + 0.9·open` (≈46–120 units).
- **Pad density and age structure:** growth attempts scale with fertility; the share of leaves on the water is the reach's cover; a low-frequency gap field opens holes in the carpet where a rhizome has died back (more where it's poor); open reaches keep broad empty water by standing the carpet back from it (negative space as a resource). - **Water clarity:** bed absorption scales `0.7–2.6×` with turbidity, tea-dark tannin in the deep colour, and caustics fade in turbid water (bed, weeds, deep leaves).

**Leaf colour by age and shade, a wider gamut:** young growth yellow-green, old leaves olive, leaves in shade cooled toward blue-green, everything a touch less saturated, so a bright leaf is an event; old leaves carry the nibbles. Passed per pad as `age` (iC.z) and a tint derived from it.

## Round 45 — critique of Round 44: gathered dew went dark

**What went wrong.** Round 44 made a lifted drop a refracting lens: its body is whatever the screen shows behind it, lightly fresnel-mixed with sky, plus a small orange ember at the centre. Over the water and the hull that "behind" is dark green and brown, so the drops flew to the lantern as beads of bronze with a dim core — darker than the dew still on the leaves, which lightens what it refracts (×1.22 + .035) and is lit by the sun. Caught in a screenshot: dark discs beside bright leaf dew. The old mote was a self-lit warm cream point with a halo; gathered dew is the *light* that feeds the lantern, and the meaning was lost with the luminance.

**What was right and is kept:** the capillary release (the drop clings to the moving leaf for 0.28 s while the leaf's own drop fades), the stretch, the arrival counted once, and the lens itself while the drop is still water.

**The fix.**
- The refracted scene is lightened as the leaf's dew lightens it (×1.25 + .06), and the drop's body has a pale floor from the moment it lets go, so it never reads as a bead of bronze.
- As it flies it turns to light: a blend toward warm gold (`flight = smoothstep(0.2, 0.75, age)`), brighter as it nears the lantern (within 140 units), with a soft halo beyond the rim the way the lantern itself has one. The ember is only for the water phase. The sprite grows a few pixels as it glows instead of shrinking to 58%.
- The chosen leaf's glow, cut from .22 to .10 in Round 44, is set at .15: a child needs to see what they have chosen.

Verified in Chromium: on the leaf, silvery water; released, pale; in flight, cream-gold points with halos, plainly brighter than the leaf dew.

## Round 46 — Rounds 44 and 45 reverted

Both the dew-as-water round (44, from the cell) and the light-in-flight fix on top of it (45) were still worse than what came before, so `client/main.ts`, `render.ts` and `shaders.ts` are restored to their state before Round 44 (nothing else had changed in them since). Once gathered it is *light*: what the lantern is fed with, the reward the child watches arrive. ## Round 47 — dew, against the original brief

The brief was: "dew drops don't feel part of the scene; make them more realistic, and more magical when appropriate." Round 44 answered it on the leaf and lost it in flight; Round 46 reverted both. This round rebuilds in two halves.

**On the leaf, water.** Kept from Round 44: the pinned contact line with the cap lagging as the leaf flexes, the canopy reflected in the drop, the finite highlight footprint. Reworked: drops are ~25% smaller (dew, not marbles); the body is the leaf seen through water — a touch darker and richer than the dry leaf, brighter at the centre where the drop focuses light, darker toward the rim where the lens compresses; a dark contact line all round, heaviest toward the sun, and a thin bright line inside the far rim where light is thrown back; the sky only at the rim and only what the canopy lets through; one sharp sun pinpoint with a small bloom instead of a broad wash; a faint wet ring on the leaf at the contact line; the drop's shadow on the leaf kept at .30.

**Magic where it means something.** A chosen leaf's drops light from within, more so at dusk (Round 44's `enchant`), and their light spills a little onto the leaf (.18); the lantern has its own pinpoint in every drop within reach, with a warm focus beneath; and gathered dew becomes light — the mote begins as the drop itself (its own size, pale, at rest as the leaf's drop fades), then warms and brightens as it lifts away to the lantern. ## Round 48 — dew shaded from photographs

Three photographs of real dew on lily pads were supplied as reference. What they show, against Round 47's pale beads: a drop on a pad is a **low, flat lens of darker, more saturated green than the leaf around it**, not a bright bead; its outline is irregular and often elongated where drops have merged; the rim is crisp, dark on the near side and a thin bright line on the far side; there is one small white pinpoint of sun and almost no sky in the body; the shadow on the leaf is slight because the drop is so flat.

## Round 49 — the lantern stops at the rower; white water lives longer and goes astern

**Lantern on its own boat.** The lamp's wash on the hull and rower was `lampAt(world)` — a 150-unit Gaussian centred on the bow, so a 110-unit boat was lit evenly bow to stern. Now `lampOnDeck(p)` in the boat shader scales that wash by where on the boat it lands: an inverse-square falloff from the lamp's hanging point over the bow (full at the bow, ~a third at the rower, near nothing at the stern), and the rower's shadow — everything aft of the figure, across the width of their shoulders and widening a little with distance, is cut by 82%. At night the bow glows, the figure is lit from in front, and the basket, coil and stern sit in the dark. The lamp's light on the water is unchanged.

**White water.** Foam in the surface sim is now carried along by the river (`uFoamFlow`: the current at the camera, per sim step, in sim uv), so it belongs to the water rather than to the place where it was churned — under way it recedes astern; at rest it drifts with everything else. Its decay is slower (.988 → .9945 per step, a half-life of ~2 s instead of ~1 s) and it spreads less (.25 → .16), so the streaks stay as streaks. The water thrown aft by the blades (`puddles`) carries froth for most of its life instead of its first half-second, so each stroke lays a white streak back along the pull.

Verified: tsc, all programs compile, 27/27 tests; headless frames at night and dusk show the lit bow and the dark stern; a slow headless stroke sequence shows the blade foam persisting and sitting astern of the moving boat.

## Round 50 — white water on a caught leaf; dragonflies and butterflies

**Foam where the blade lands on a leaf.** The drive loop only churned foam when the blade was in open water (`!padAt`), and a leaf under the blade got a trickle at its edge (5-unit impulses at a 10 Hz chance). Now a caught leaf froths at its rim toward the blade every step (`0.6·k·drive·into`, scattered over ±0.55 rad just outside the rim), and the thrown-water streak (`puddles`) is shed whether or not the blade is on a leaf. The white shows at the rim rather than under the leaf, which is what a flooded edge looks like; the leaf physics is unchanged.

**Critters.** The fireflies are points of light that come and go and by day are all but gone, so the river had no creature in the air that a child could follow. `critters.ts` adds two dragonflies and three butterflies (one and two under reduced motion), drawn by a new instanced sprite program (`CRITTER_VS/FS`) above everything, each with a sun-thrown shadow on whatever is beneath it, and lifted by the same parallax as the fireflies.

- *Dragonfly*: long straight dashes at 95–145 units/s, a dead stop and a hover with a fine tremor and the head turning, a perch (2.5–7 s) on the rim of a leaf without dew or on a bud with the wings flat, and one dash in five to hang over the boat's bow. Three species by seed: an emperor's sky blue, a darter's red, a metallic green; a segmented abdomen with a dark dorsal stripe, great eyes, four clear veined wings with a pterostigma at each tip, blurred in flight. It flies on into dusk.
- *Butterfly*: a slow beat (6.5–9 Hz) the body lurches with, a wandering heading that keeps bending back toward where it is going, two in five legs aimed at an open flower and one in ten at the boat, where it settles (3–9 s) — on a flower, a leaf rim, or the boat's gunwale or bow, riding along — and opens and closes its wings at leisure. Four species: small tortoiseshell, cabbage white, common blue, brimstone, each with its own marks and a paler underside that shows as the wings lift. They roost as the light goes.
- A touch's wind flushes either from its perch; neither ever sits on a leaf the child is counting.

Verified: tsc, all programs compile, 27/27 tests; headless frames show a perched dragonfly on a leaf rim and three butterflies in flight, readable at phone scale with their shadows. Their motion, and whether they distract from the counting, need a device look.

## Round 51 — taps that didn't take; critters with height and less toy in them

**Taps on a dewy leaf sometimes landed on the water.** Two causes found. (1) The hit test was exact: a leaf counted only if the touch point fell inside 97% of its radius, and a small leaf (r ≈ 22) at phone zoom is a 35 px disc, so a fingertip's centroid a few pixels off the edge missed — and a miss on open water *cleared the child's whole selection*. Now `hitOrNear`: off every leaf, the nearest leaf with dew whose edge is within a fingertip's slack (14 CSS px, scaled by zoom) counts as touched; it is used for the pointer-down only, so drawing across leaves is unchanged. (2) One pointer at a time: a second finger while one was down was ignored, correctly — but if the first pointer's up was lost (it happens on phones) every tap after it died. A new touch now takes over when the tracked one is older than 1.5 s and not steering or dragging. Not changed: the 2.2 s lock after a solve and 1.2 s after an overshoot, during which a tapped leaf bobs but is not chosen — that is by design, but it is the other thing a child would call "didn't take".

**Critters.** Smaller (dragonfly 8–9.5, butterfly 6–7.2), and the flat colours and hard edges that read as toys reworked: bodies desaturated and shaded as cylinders with the sun on one side; the dragonfly's abdomen pinched at the waist and clubbed at the tip with fine joint rings and a dorsal stripe, pale thoracic stripes, glossy eyes, and wings that are a smoky clear membrane with a costa, cross veins and longitudinals, a pterostigma, and a soft band of sun (with a faint iridescence) that walks along the membrane as it tilts; in flight the wings are two soft fans. Butterfly wings reshaped — a fore wing with a squarer tip and a sweeping trailing edge, a scalloped hind wing — with radiating veins, darker scales at the root and margin, muted species colours, an underside that shows as the wings close, and wings that bank into a turn (the inside wing lifts more).

**Height.** They now fly in three dimensions: each has a height, a target height and a vertical velocity. A dragonfly's dash climbs or drops (low over the water hunting, high over the leaves passing through, higher still over the boat), it springs up as it leaves a perch and settles down onto one; a butterfly rises on each stroke and sinks between about a cruising height that drifts leg to leg, comes down toward a flower and climbs well up when it leaves. Height drives parallax (position and size), and the shadow's throw, softness and strength.

Verified: tsc, all programs compile, 27/27 tests; frames show a perched dragonfly on a leaf rim, a tortoiseshell in flight and a cabbage white settled on a lily, with heights logged from 0.09 (perched) to 2.1 (a dragonfly passing high).

## Round 52 — the drips are silenced

The stroke had three sounds: the dip at the catch, the gurgle at the release, and the drips through the recovery — water running off the lifted blades and the spray thrown at the release. The dip and gurgle are mostly filtered noise and read as water. Each drip was an 18–43 ms grain with a sine tone at a random 900–1600 Hz, and a stroke produced twenty-odd of them in a scatter over a second and a half: a run of glass notes on top of the water, and the thing that sounded wrong. The drips are no longer voiced (`main.ts`: the `'drip'` sound kind is dropped; the fish-rise plink, which used the same sound, is dropped too). Their ripples still land. The dip and gurgle are unchanged.

**How to bring them back properly.** Water running off an oar is not a row of notes. It is (a) a fine, untoned patter — tiny noise ticks at 2–4 kHz with no pitch, dense at first and thinning as the blade drains, at a level well under the dip; and (b) one or two larger drops with a real *bloop*: a short low noise burst whose *frequency sweeps up* (the cavity a drop opens closes with a rising pitch, ~600→1200 Hz over 30 ms), never a fixed sine. The count should be small (three or four per blade, not eighteen), timed to the blades' actual height (most in the first 300 ms after the release), and panned with the blade. Try (a) alone first; add (b) only for a hard pull.

## Round 53 — telling the stroke's sounds apart

Silencing the drips (Round 52) did not remove the sound in question, and nothing was missed, so the drips were not it. With the worklet live a stroke has: the **dip** (catch), the **gurgle** (release), and, only when the hull or a blade shoulders a leaf, a **knock**; with the worklet unavailable each of these is a bandpassed noise burst instead (`fallbackSplash`) that sounds quite different. For finding the culprit by ear: `?mute=dip`, `?mute=gurgle`, `?mute=knock` (comma-separated, also `plop`) silence a named sound, and `window.__stillwater.audio` reports `worklet` / `fallback` / `off`.

## Round 54 — no gurgle; a gentle splash for a tap

By ear with `?mute=`, the sound that jarred was the **gurgle** at the release: its three pitched droplets read as plinks, and it sat as a second event after the pull. It is no longer voiced; the stroke is the dip and then the water. (The fast finger-drag through the water used the same gurgle; it now makes a soft plop instead.)

The **tap on water** was the same mistake in a different coat: a 900 Hz grain plus a 1500 Hz grain carrying a 450–800 Hz sine. It is now a gentle splash with no pitch anywhere: a soft rounded push of water (520 Hz cutoff, 170 ms), a short breath of spray (1600 Hz, 70 ms), and four faint wet ticks scattered over the next 150 ms.

## Round 55 — white water over a flooded leaf
