# Stillwater — making the learning land (and feel good)

2026-09-28 · proposal · the interface, environment and feel are locked; everything
below works through the mechanics that already exist (dew on leaves, touching leaves
to gather, the boat carried on by a solve, sound, light, blooms, fireflies), or adds
the smallest possible piece to them.

---

## 1. Where the learning loop is today

**The loop.**
- The pond shows a target (dots or a numeral).
- The child touches dewy leaves until the drops make it.
- The drops lift, the boat is carried on, and a new ask arrives about 3 s later.

**What is already right.**
- **Proficiency is continuous.** Since Round 20 it is one number (`mastery` 0..1) that biases an overlapping field of five activities: gather, add, more, groups, times.
- **Earlier forms never vanish.** That is interleaving, which is good for maths (see §3).
- **The ask is always solvable from what's visible.** It is drawn from the dew ahead, and repaired only once the child has started.
- **Nothing fails.** "Over" lets drops slide back, and "mismatch" restarts the selection from the new leaf.
- **Any valid answer counts.** For groups, 3 leaves of 6 or 6 leaves of 3 are both accepted.

**What's missing** (and what Candy Crush and the learning science both point at):

| gap | consequence |
|---|---|
| One scalar for all of maths | The pond can't know that *7 = 3 + 4* is shaky while *5 + 5* is solid, so there is no spacing and no targeted review. |
| No fluency signal | A child who counts every drop on the leaf and one who *knows* 3 + 4 look identical. Fluency is the goal of number-fact practice, not just accuracy. |
| Every solve feels the same | Same lift, same propel, same phrase. No escalation, no surprise, no "that was a good one". |
| No arc | It is endless by design (keep that), but there is no rhythm of tension and release, which is what makes Candy Crush sessions feel shaped. |
| One kind of ask per activity | "Make n" is the whole vocabulary. Candy Crush keeps one gesture (swap) fresh for thousands of levels by varying the *objective*. |
| Help is words ("leaves that match") | It is late, and it tells rather than shows. |

## 2. What Candy Crush actually does, and what transfers

Candy Crush is worth studying because it is a *single, trivially simple gesture*
(swap two candies) sustained for years of play. It doesn't get that from the gesture.
It gets it from what's layered around the gesture. Separating the parts that transfer
from the parts we must never copy:

### Transfers (reframed for a meditative river)

1. **Teach by doing, one new thing at a time.** New elements arrive in a level built
   around them, nearly impossible to fail, with a one-line prompt at most. Then they
   are combined with things already known.
   *For us:* when the proficiency field first brings in a new activity (e.g.
   groups), the next few asks should be the *easiest possible instance* of it, laid
   out in the clearest way (two leaves of two, side by side). Then they fade back into
   the mix.

2. **The sawtooth difficulty curve.** Difficulty is not a ramp. A hard level is
   followed by a few easy "relief" levels, which feel like mastery and pay back the
   effort.
   *For us:* shape stretches of river (see P3) so a harder ask is followed by quick,
   easy ones. Today asks are sampled independently, so the rhythm is random noise.

3. **Juice: feedback scaled to achievement.** A match of 3 gives a pop, 4 makes a
   striped candy, 5 makes a colour bomb, and "Sweet / Tasty / Divine" escalate.
   Feedback is proportional and legible.
   *For us:* no words, no numbers flying. Instead, sound and light that grow with
   the elegance of an answer and with a run of good ones (P4, P6). The river's own
   instruments are the juice: note, bloom, firefly, lantern.

4. **Cascades: generous luck.** After your move, falling candies sometimes make
   matches you didn't plan. You feel lucky and clever at once, and it breaks up the
   grind.
   *For us:* after a solve, the lifting drops occasionally shake dew loose from a
   flower onto a nearby dry leaf, completing an instant easy follow-up ("a gift from
   the river"). It is rare, never needed, and always a pleasure.

5. **The end-of-level celebration (Sugar Crush).** When a level ends, every leftover
   move becomes a bonus explosion. It is a free, big, satisfying moment that closes
   the arc.
   *For us:* at the end of a stretch (P3), the dewy leaves still in view each chime
   their count as their dew rises into fireflies, low to high. It is a counting
   rehearsal disguised as a finale.

6. **Near-miss framing.** On a failed level Candy Crush tells you exactly how close
   you were ("only 1 jelly left").
   *For us:* there is no failure, but "over" can *show* the surplus. The extra drops
   visibly slide back into the water one by one (count them: two too many) instead of
   the word line "let a few drops return".

7. **Objective variety on one gesture.** Clear the jelly, bring down the ingredients,
   reach a score, free the animals: same swap, different reasons.
   *For us:* the same touch-leaves gesture, with different asks (P5).

8. **Boosters when stuck.** Help is offered at the moment of frustration, and it
   feels like a power rather than a correction.
   *For us:* progressive, visual scaffolds (P7). The river helps; it never tells.

### Never copy (these are why Candy Crush is also a cautionary tale)

- **Lives, energy, waiting to play again.** They exist to monetise impatience.
- **Timers and move limits as pressure.** Timed maths is a known driver of maths
  anxiety (Boaler, 2014). Tension belongs to the *puzzle*, never the clock.
- **Streaks that can be lost, daily-login guilt, FOMO.** Loss aversion aimed at
  children.
- **Currencies, loot, variable-ratio reward schedules used to compel.** Variable
  delight is fine *inside* a session; variable reward as a hook to come back is not.
- **Levels tuned to be failed** to sell boosters.
- **Fail screens.**

The line we hold: **variable delight, never variable pressure.**

## 3. The learning science that matters here

- **Retrieval practice (the testing effect).** Recalling a fact strengthens it far
  more than re-seeing it (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006). Every ask is a retrieval, as
  long as the child *recalls* 3 + 4 rather than counting seven drops.
- **Spacing.** Reviews spread over expanding intervals beat massed practice, robustly
  and across ages (Cepeda et al., 2006). This is the "spaced repetition" in the
  brief. It needs *item-level* memory, which we don't have yet (P1).
- **Interleaving.** Mixing problem types beats blocked practice for maths, because
  learners must choose the strategy, not only execute it (Rohrer & Taylor, 2007). We
  already do this; keep it, and make it deliberate with contrast pairs (3 + 4 next to
  3 × 4).
- **Desirable difficulties.** Spacing, interleaving, varied representation and
  retrieval all *feel* harder in the moment and produce better long-term learning
  (Bjork, 1994). That is why the juice matters: it pays for the difficulty.
- **The ~85% zone.** Learning is fastest when about 15% of attempts are errors
  (Wilson et al., 2019). It is also flow's challenge–skill balance. Our "errors" are
  gentle friction (over, mismatch), and the difficulty controller should aim for
  roughly 80–85% friction-free first attempts.
- **Concreteness fading.** Move from concrete to iconic to abstract representations
  (Fyfe et al., 2014). The target display already does this (line → frames → array
  → numeral). The *dew layout* does not (see P8).
- **Subitizing and number bonds.** Recognising small quantities at a glance, and
  seeing 6 as 3 + 3, underpin early arithmetic (Clements, 1999). Fact fluency grows
  from *derived-fact strategies* (make ten, doubles, near doubles), not rote recall
  (Baroody, 2006).
- **Motivation.** Autonomy, competence and relatedness predict enjoyment of games
  (Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski, 2006). Stillwater is strong on autonomy (steer, drift,
  choose any valid leaves). Competence feedback is where it is thin.

## 4. Proposals

Ranked by learning value against effort. **Within mechanics** = only the rules or
tuning change. **Tiny addition** = one new visual or sound in the existing idiom.

### P1 — The river remembers: item-level facts with spaced retrieval (within mechanics · high value)

Keep `mastery` as the broad field. Under it, add a small memory of **facts**:

- sum facts as unordered pairs/triples (`3+4`, `5+5`, `2+3+4`);
- groups as `k×m` (with commutativity, so `3×4 ≡ 4×3` but tracked as having been
  *seen* both ways).

For each fact, store `{ strength, lastSeen, halfLife, fluentCount }`. Use a
half-life-regression or Leitner-box style model (Settles & Meeder, 2016): a clean,
quick recall roughly doubles the half-life, and friction halves it.

**How the pond "asks" for a due fact without breaking the ecology.** The pond grows
leaves upstream all the time. `dewFor` already decides each new leaf's drops. When a
fact is due, bias the dew of leaves *being grown ahead* so the parts of that fact
appear (e.g. a 3 and a 4 a few leaves apart). By the time they drift into view,
`chooseActivity` finds that `7` is makeable and prefers due facts among the
makeable targets. Spacing is planted as ecology, not imposed as a quiz.

Intervals are expanding within a session (≈1, 3, 8, 20 min) and carry across days via
the existing localStorage save. Session start reviews what's due first, softly.

### P2 — Silent fluency: read *how* they solved it (within mechanics · high value)

Record, invisibly:
- **latency:** target shown to first touch, and to solve;
- **path:** which leaves, in what order, and whether a leaf was chosen then let go.

- **Counting vs recall.** Quick on small counts, then slower per extra drop, means
  counting. A flat, quick time regardless of size means recall. Fluency is what moves
  a fact's half-life (P1) and what `learn()` should weigh, alongside friction.
- **Strategy.** Picking the two-leaf answer when a four-leaf one was nearer means the
  child *sees the bond*. That is worth more than any count.

**Never shown.** No timers, no speed rewards. The point is to keep the difficulty in
the 80–85% band (§3) and let the pond know what to plant.

### P3 — Stretches: a sawtooth arc inside the endless river (within mechanics · high value)

Group asks into **stretches** of about 4–7, with a shape:

1. **Warm-up.** One or two easy, fluent facts (confidence).
2. **Reach.** One or two asks at the edge (a due-but-shaky fact, or a new form
   introduced in its clearest instance).
3. **Consolidate.** The same idea, slightly varied (the same fact in a different
   layout, or its partner: 3 + 4 then 4 + 3, 3 × 4 then 4 × 3).
4. **Relief.** Two or three quick easy ones (Candy Crush's post-hard-level breather).
5. **Finale** (P6).

The world already has the punctuation: a **river bend or an open pool** marks the end
of a stretch. The channel meanders anyway, so time stretches to widenings of the
channel. No UI and no levels, just a rhythm the child *feels*. Stretch length and the
height of "reach" adapt to P2.

### P4 — Elegance is noticed: specials without points (tiny addition · high value)

When an answer is *better*, the river answers more richly. Candy Crush's 4-match
striped candy becomes, here, the pond noticing a clever move:

| elegant answer | what it teaches | the river's answer |
|---|---|---|
| fewest leaves possible (e.g. 10 = 6 + 4, not 3 + 3 + 4) | number bonds, efficient decomposition | a lily bud on one of those leaves opens as the dew lifts |
| making ten on the way (7 + 3 + 2 chosen 7, 3 first) | the bridge-through-ten strategy | a single low chime at the 10 before the final note |
| a double (4 + 4) | doubles facts | the two leaves drift together and touch |
| groups the other way round (4 × 3 after 3 × 4) | commutativity | twin blooms on the two groupings |
| an unhesitating answer (P2 fast and clean) | fluency | the lantern brightens a little and stays so for the stretch |

This works within the existing blooms, lantern, sound and pad physics. Nothing is
counted or scored, but the child learns what "a good one" looks like because the
world responds.

### P5 — One gesture, many asks (within mechanics · high value)

The same touch-the-leaves gesture, and the same target dots, carries different
reasons to gather. Each is drawn from the proficiency field like today's activities:

- **Bond:** "make 8 *with two leaves*". The dot line splits into two tinted halves
  as leaves are chosen. Forces decomposition over counting-on.
- **Missing part:** the boat has already *caught* a leaf. The oar or hull holds it
  pressed (the physics exists: `caught`, `sink`), its drops count into the target,
  and the child finds the rest ("you have 5; make 9").
- **Make ten:** the target is always 10 for a stretch, and each answer must be
  different (4 + 6, then 3 + 7…). The frames display already shows ten.
- **Double:** "two matching leaves", i.e. groups with k = 2.
- **Both ways:** after a groups solve, the next ask is the same product, answered the
  other way round (P4 rewards it).
- **Compare** (early, gather band): "the leaf with more dew". A one-touch ask that
  exercises magnitude comparison and subitizing.

### P6 — The finale: dew becomes fireflies, counting out loud (tiny addition · medium value)

At the end of a stretch (P3), every dewy leaf in view **chimes its count**: that many
soft notes, the drops rising one per note as fireflies, leaf by leaf, low to high
(the `note()` voice exists). This is the Sugar Crush moment in the river's voice. It
is a free, generous release, and a rehearsal of subitizing and counting that asks
nothing of the child.

### P7 — The river helps, and never tells (within mechanics · medium value)

Replace late word hints with a **progressive visual scaffold** keyed to hesitation
(P2), fading as proficiency grows:

1. After ~6 s of no touch, the dew on *valid* leaves glints once (the specular
   exists).
2. After ~12 s, one leaf of a valid answer sways toward the boat.
3. After ~20 s, its partner glints too.

Scaffolding used on a fact lowers its P1 credit, silently. Words stay for the very
first ask and for screen readers (the live region).

### P8 — Dew layouts that fade from pattern to scatter (within mechanics · medium value)

`layDrops` scatters at random. For low proficiency, and for a fact's early
exposures, lay drops in **recognisable patterns**:
- dice faces for 1–6;
- 5 as a quincunx;
- 6 as two rows of three;
- 7 as 5 and 2.

These patterns support perceptual and *conceptual* subitizing (seeing 6 as 3 + 3).
Fade to natural scatter as the fact's strength rises (concreteness fading). A
scattered 6 then becomes a desirable difficulty: the child must *structure* it
themselves.

### P9 — Welcome back, never guilt (within mechanics · low effort)

On return after a gap, the first stretch is warm-up plus due reviews (P1), with an
extra bloom or two in view, so the river is visibly *glad*. There are no streaks to
lose and no "you missed a day". Spacing across days is the learning benefit;
the welcome is the only motivational lever.

### Optional, only if the lock allows one visible addition

- **The rope as an abacus.** The painter line (which has no mechanic yet) could carry
  a small wooden bead for each fact that reaches fluency, grouped in fives by colour
  like a rekenrek. It would be a quiet, physical record of the journey that trails
  behind the boat, never a score in the sky. It gives the rope a purpose and the child
  a sense of accumulation without a meter.

## 5. How we'd know it's working

Extend the existing anonymous `/report` channel with coarse, per-session aggregates.
No identity, no raw touches.

- **First-try (friction-free) rate** per activity: aim for 80–85%.
- **Median latency by fact size:** the counting slope should flatten over sessions
  (recall replacing counting).
- **Fact half-lives** growing across days (spacing working).
- **Share of fewest-leaf answers** in sum asks (bonds being seen).
- **Session length and return rate:** a sanity check only, *never* optimised for
  (that is the Candy Crush trap).

---

## 6. Suggested order of work

1. **P2 + P1 core.** Record latency and path, and build the fact memory model. Pure
   and testable in `numeracy.ts`, like today's rules. Nothing visible changes yet.
2. **P3 stretches.** The sawtooth, driven by P1 and P2, with bends as punctuation.
3. **P5 asks** (bond, missing part, make ten, both ways) and **P8 layouts**.
4. **Juice: P4 elegance responses and P6 finale**, tuned by ear and eye in the
   locked idiom.
5. **P7 scaffolds**, replacing hint words.
6. **P9 welcome back.** Optionally the rope abacus.

Each step ships on its own, with the numeracy tests extended (they are pure
functions).
